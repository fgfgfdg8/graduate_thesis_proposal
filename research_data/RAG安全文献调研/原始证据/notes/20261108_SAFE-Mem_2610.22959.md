## Research question and method

SAFE-Mem addresses authority laundering in persistent LLM-agent memory: an external artifact may be an accurate source for what it said, but should not acquire the principal's authority as a preference, rule, trust decision, or permission. The adversary controls artifact contents but cannot forge runtime channel records, write the memory store directly, alter another principal's turns, or access component prompts/weights. A principal can still explicitly endorse a claim; this is a stated boundary of the model.

The system stores source sentences, extracted claims, and entities in a provenance graph. Each claim has a kind `kappa`, authority `alpha`, and party scope `s`; its effective use level is `lambda = min(kappa, alpha)`. External-artifact claims receive description-level authority, while authority elevation requires a principal-originated retention request and is capped by the kind requested. A query-only LLM classifier determines the needed use and target. SAFE-Mem filters claims by level and scope before similarity ranking, checks same-subject/relation contradictions by authority, returns claims plus admissible passages, wraps retrieved content in runtime-generated level markers, and checks identifiers again at tool-call time. Inadmissible content is retained as a source record rather than deleted.

This is a provenance/authorization control, not a content-truth detector or poison classifier. Its core guarantee is conditional on trusted channel metadata, correct extraction/use classification, and no principal endorsement; it does not establish whether a described external assertion is factually true.

## Evaluation read from the paper

- **MPBench:** 3,241 attack cases and 2,999 benign cases are described; 2,999 attack cases are scored, comprising 2,893 distinct retrieval queries for clustered bootstrap analysis. The reported SAFE-Mem ASR/Recall/Restraint (%) across GPT-5.6-terra, GPT-4o-mini, Claude Haiku 5.5, and Qwen3.8-Flash is `2.6/86.3/48.4`, `6.2/85.9/50.6`, `1.5/84.3/51.4`, and `1.9/83.1/49.2`. The corresponding no-defense ASR is `26.7`, `49.8`, `10.9`, and `41.1`.
- **Write/retrieve separation:** injected memory is still written in 38.0% of GPT-5.6-terra and 57.6% of GPT-4o-mini attack cases, but is retrieved into the attack-query context in 1.1% and 2.8%, versus 30.2% and 55.7% without defense. This is the central mechanism: preserve source records, constrain their later use.
- **LoCoMo:** answer accuracy is `61.3`, `54.6`, `53.5`, and `47.0` versus no-defense `50.2`, `39.5`, `50.4`, and `44.2`; these are gains of 11.1, 15.1, 3.1, and 2.8 percentage points.
- **EHRAgent under MINJA:** SAFE-Mem's generated-code ASR is still 72.4%, but executed-attack rate is 0%; benign retrieved-memory loss is 2.0%. Do not summarize the 72.4% ASR column as successful execution. Removing the scope check raises attack execution to 44.4% in the paper's ablation.
- **Cost and judgment:** the full MPBench run uses about 18-20% more LLM calls than no defense, versus 88% more for A-MemGuard. Human validation reports 84.0% agreement for retention judgments (kappa 0.76), 84.7%/68.7% exact use-kind agreement for GPT-5.6-terra/GPT-4o-mini, and 83.5% answer-judge agreement (kappa 0.67).
- **Denominator note:** the paper distinguishes 3,241 attack cases from the 2,999 scored cases and identifies 2,893 distinct queries in the bootstrap analysis. Reconcile the omitted-case rule and released benchmark rows before recomputing ASR or comparing denominators; the reported tables use the 2,999 scored cases.

## Relevance to the proposal

- **B, strong method and scope neighbor:** directly overlaps source provenance, persistent memory poisoning, governance-aware retrieval, and preserving benign memory while suppressing later misuse. It constrains broad novelty claims such as “first provenance-aware memory defense,” “first retrieval-time use control,” or “first security mechanism that preserves legitimate recall.” It should be compared as an authorization/provenance baseline if the proposal's deployment includes persistent agent memory.
- **A, indirect only:** it stores source/claim/entity relations, but the paper does not study a real long source processed by an attacker-unknown victim chunker, complementary payload-bearing chunks co-retrieved by one normal query, per-chunk joint necessity, or a real external RAG generation endpoint.
- **B, not equivalent:** it retains artifacts and applies per-use provenance authorization; it neither identifies malicious false-fact chunks nor calibrates independent clean-query final query-level OR deletion risk. Its reported 2.0% benign memory loss on EHRAgent is not that query-level calibration guarantee.
- **Threat-model boundary:** authority laundering and cross-principal scope are not the same as false facts that do not borrow authority. The authors explicitly leave false external claims and cases where a principal is persuaded to endorse content outside their defense guarantee; SAFE-Mem complements rather than replaces content-factuality checks.
